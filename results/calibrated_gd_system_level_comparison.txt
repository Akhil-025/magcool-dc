Mean |COP shift| across 6 checked device(s): 36.6% (this average is dominated by outliers -- see next line).
IMPORTANT, NOT JUST A SMALL ACCURACY SHIFT: 2/6 device(s) (Tusek_singlebed_Gd_2010, DTU_Eriksen_rotary_Gd_2015) go from a real, positive Qc under plain GADOLINIUM to a HARD ZERO under GADOLINIUM_CALIBRATED at the SAME mdot/span/field -- the grain-Tc broadening pushes the structural feasibility margin (2*dTad_noload - span, core/amr_cycle.py's cooling_capacity()) negative at these operating points, where plain GADOLINIUM's sharper (unbroadened) peak kept it just barely positive. This is real, concrete evidence -- not just the performance-cost argument above -- for why GADOLINIUM_CALIBRATED is NOT swapped in as the system-wide default: doing so would silently turn currently-working design points into 'infeasible', for reasons unrelated to any actual physical infeasibility of those devices (they demonstrably work -- that's why they're in this benchmark set).
CONCLUSION: this is the measured cost of NOT wiring GADOLINIUM_CALIBRATED into core/amr_cycle.py's system-level default -- read it before deciding whether the ~6-19x per-call performance cost AND the feasibility-zeroing risk above (see this function's own docstring) are worth accepting for your use case. The current default (plain GADOLINIUM system-wide, GADOLINIUM_CALIBRATED only in run_validation()'s material-level report) is deliberate, not an oversight.
