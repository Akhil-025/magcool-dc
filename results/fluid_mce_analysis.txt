CONCLUSION: at realistic particle loadings (phi swept up to 0.63, the random-close-packing ceiling), the mixture-heat-capacity dilution model (core/fluid_mce_cycle.py physics item 1) combined with this architecture's lack of regeneration (physics item 3) collapses the usable span to a few Kelvin (0.66K at phi=0.20 in this specific comparison) -- dramatically less than solid AMR achieves at the same field/flow, whose regenerator bed amplifies achievable span well beyond a single stage's own dTad (see core/amr_cycle.py's own characteristic-curve discussion). Notably, the ferrofluid system's own COP_electrical (78.0) is actually HIGHER than the solid AMR's (6.5) at this shared, ultra-narrow span -- because W_parasitic (Darcy-Weisbach pipe flow) is small relative to W_mag at this flow rate, and both systems' COP formulas have Qc largely cancel out of the ratio -- but BOTH trail the liquid-cooling and vapor-compression baselines at this span (see the numbers above). This COP comparison should not be read as 'ferrofluid MCE beats solid AMR' in general: it holds only at the ferrofluid system's own tiny achievable span, which the solid AMR could trivially also hit (and does, with far more Qc); the real headline result is the SPAN, not this specific COP comparison. This is a genuine, unforced finding from this pass's own model, not assumed going in. As HONESTY FLAG #2 in core/fluid_mce_cycle.py states, this is a design-exploration/comparison tool, not a validated result -- no benchmark magnetocaloric-fluid-as-working-body refrigeration device was found in this project's corpus or this pass's own literature search.
