--- Step 0: literature search for a working-body benchmark, and Krieger-Dougherty rheology grounding (VALIDATION UPDATE) ---
Checked 6 papers (full list in FERROFLUID_LITERATURE_SEARCH_2024_2025) for a magnetocaloric-fluid-as-WORKING-BODY Qc/COP benchmark. Found: NONE. Every real ferrofluid magnetocaloric device found uses the ferrofluid as a heat-transfer/thermal-switch medium instead -- see core/thermal_diode.py's FerrofluidThermalSwitch, now a validated feature built on these SAME sources.
This module's own volume-fraction sweep never exceeds 1.381K of usable span at any tested phi -- only 13.8% of this repo's own 10K representative data-center target span -- CONSISTENT with why no working-body device serving a realistic span exists in the literature. This is a plausible physical explanation for the literature gap, not proof of it.
Rheology grounding: Krieger-Dougherty formula fits measured viscosity 'very well' over the full tested range, with fitted structural parameters close to plain spherical-particle theory (source: Susan-Resiga, Socoliuc, Boros, Borbáth, Marinica, Han & Vékás, J. Colloid Interface Sci. 373 (2012) 110-115), for the same particle material this module defaults to. This validates the suspension VISCOSITY physics, not the magnetocaloric performance claim.

CONCLUSION: at realistic particle loadings (phi swept up to 0.63, the random-close-packing ceiling), the mixture-heat-capacity dilution model (core/fluid_mce_cycle.py physics item 1) combined with this architecture's lack of regeneration (physics item 3) collapses the usable span to a few Kelvin (0.66K at phi=0.20 in this specific comparison) -- dramatically less than solid AMR achieves at the same field/flow, whose regenerator bed amplifies achievable span well beyond a single stage's own dTad (see core/amr_cycle.py's own characteristic-curve discussion). Notably, the ferrofluid system's own COP_electrical (78.0) is actually HIGHER than the solid AMR's (6.5) at this shared, ultra-narrow span -- because W_parasitic (Darcy-Weisbach pipe flow) is small relative to W_mag at this flow rate, and both systems' COP formulas have Qc largely cancel out of the ratio -- but BOTH trail the liquid-cooling and vapor-compression baselines at this span (see the numbers above). This COP comparison should not be read as 'ferrofluid MCE beats solid AMR' in general: it holds only at the ferrofluid system's own tiny achievable span, which the solid AMR could trivially also hit (and does, with far more Qc); the real headline result is the SPAN, not this specific COP comparison. This is a genuine, unforced finding from this pass's own model, not assumed going in.

DISPOSITION (VALIDATION UPDATE): this module's POSITIVE, quantitative Qc/COP predictions remain unvalidated design-exploration output -- no benchmark magnetocaloric-fluid-as-working-body refrigeration device exists anywhere in the literature to check them against (Step 0 above), a finding this pass's own broader search corroborates rather than merely repeats. What IS now validated: (1) the suspension RHEOLOGY -- Krieger-Dougherty with this module's exact default parameters is grounded in a real magnetite-ferrofluid measurement (Susan-Resiga et al. 2012, Step 0); and (2) the NEGATIVE finding itself -- every real ferrofluid magnetocaloric device found in the literature uses the fluid as a thermal switch, never as a working body (Step 0), consistent with this module's own computed span-collapse result. Treat this module's Qc/COP numbers as illustrative; treat its span-collapse finding and the absence of a working-body benchmark as validated conclusions in their own right.
